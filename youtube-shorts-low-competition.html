YouTube Shorts

How to find lower-competition Shorts topics.

The best opportunity is often not a zero-competition topic. It is a topic where viewer interest is growing faster than the supply of useful videos.

Start with a question people actually ask

Shorts can benefit from search behavior as well as feed distribution. “How does X work?”, “Is X worth it?”, “X vs Y” and “3 mistakes with X” are useful structures because they map to clear viewer intent.

Go one level narrower

A broad topic attracts broad competition. Narrowing by audience, price, use case, location, feature or result can create a more defensible angle. The narrower version should still be understandable without extra context.

Check whether existing results answer the question well

Competition is not just the number of videos. Ten weak or outdated videos can leave more room than two excellent ones. Look for missing examples, old information, unclear explanations or topics where comments keep asking for follow-ups.

Use freshness to your advantage

Software updates, product releases, policy changes, new research and emerging creator formats can create short windows where older videos no longer answer the current question.

Build for retention, not keyword stuffing

A useful keyword may help the topic get understood, but the short still needs to hold attention. Move quickly from the hook to proof, demonstration or explanation. Do not repeat the title for ten seconds before delivering value.

Measure after publishing

Use performance to refine your topic selection. Strong retention with weak reach may suggest packaging or distribution issues. Strong reach with poor retention may mean the topic is attractive but the execution does not deliver.
